Logistics Job Cards: Why Excel Breaks and What to Use Instead

Almost every small logistics company starts with a spreadsheet and a WhatsApp group. It works — until the business grows and nobody is sure which version of the sheet is right.

By FadeFlow TeamPublished 3 Nov 20263 min read

What is a logistics job card?

A job card is the single record of one logistics job: the customer, the route or shipment details, the supplier and agent involved, the status and every cost along the way. When it’s complete, anyone in the team can answer “where are we with this job?” without asking around.

How most small logistics companies run jobs today

Typically, a request arrives on WhatsApp or by phone. Someone adds a row to an Excel sheet. Updates are shared in a group chat. Expenses are noted on paper or in a separate file. At month-end, someone tries to put it all together.

Five places where Excel breaks

  1. Versions. jobs_final_v3.xlsx on one laptop, jobs_final_v4.xlsx on another. Nobody knows which is right.
  2. Status is unclear. A cell that says “ongoing” was last updated three days ago — or was it?
  3. Expenses are disconnected. Trip and operational costs live somewhere else, so you only learn a job lost money weeks later.
  4. Suppliers and agents are scattered. Who handled which job, at what cost, is buried in chats.
  5. No history. When a customer asks about a job from last year, finding it takes an afternoon.

What a job card system should do

  • Create one job card per job, with a clear status from draft to ongoing to completed.
  • Link every job to its customer, supplier and agent.
  • Record trip and operational expenses against the job, so margin is visible.
  • Show the whole team what is pending, ongoing and done.
  • Keep a searchable history of every job.

Switching without disrupting operations

  1. Start with new jobs only — don’t try to migrate years of history on day one.
  2. Add your active customers, suppliers and agents first.
  3. Agree as a team that job status is updated in one place only.
  4. Review open jobs together weekly for the first month.
